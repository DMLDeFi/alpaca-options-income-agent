The Problem

Humans aren't bad at options trading — they're bad at discipline

Options reward consistency under stress: tracking volatility, sizing positions, and enforcing risk limits continuously, without emotion. Most traders don't lack strategy knowledge — they override their own rules the moment a position moves against them.

What breaks a human trader

Letting a loser run "just a bit longer," closing winners too early out of anxiety, and drifting position sizes up after a good week — none of it is a knowledge problem.

What an agent is built for

Apply the same risk gate to trade #1 and trade #100. Never negotiate with a stop. React the instant a limit is breached, at 3am or 3pm, every single time.

How It Works

Scan → Risk Gate → Reason → Execute

Every cycle runs the same four-stage pipeline, autonomously, several times a day.

01

Scan the universe

Pulls live option chains across 15 liquid names and builds defined-risk credit spreads sized off each name's own realized volatility.

02

Hard risk gates

Every candidate is filtered by max-loss, total-exposure, and position-count caps before anything else sees it.

03

Claude reasons

What survives the gates is judged by Claude for trade quality, data sanity, and portfolio-level redundancy.

04

Execute on Alpaca

Approved trades go out as net-credit limit orders through Alpaca's official CLI — the same tool built for unattended agents.
